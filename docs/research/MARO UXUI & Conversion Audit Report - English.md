## Summary Before the Details

The website has a **relatively clear visual identity**, but its current UX is not built around the customer's most important question:

> **"What do you offer? How much does it cost? What does it look like? And where do I order it or visit?"**

The biggest issue is that the website devotes enormous space to the brand story, sections, whitespace, and navigation, while **the product itself—the menu—is the weakest part of the experience**.

More seriously, the menu hides each item's price behind a click, shows no product image or description in the list, and offers a very sparse detail modal. As a result, users cannot even compare three drinks quickly.

The current homepage is arranged as follows: Hero → Offers → Branches → Menu preview → About → Full Menu, with Talabat for ordering and Google Maps for branches. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

# UX/UI + Conversion Audit

## 1. Prices Are Hidden from Users in the Menu

**Location:** Full Menu — Screenshots 9, 10, 11  
**Type:** Menu / UX / Conversion  
**Priority:** **Critical**  
**Scope:** Systemic

Almost every product row says:

**"Tap to view price"**

Instead of displaying the actual price.

This is one of the website's biggest problems.

### Why Is This a Problem?

Café customers do not browse a menu as if it were a luxury product catalog. They typically do this:

Coffee → 45  
Latte → 65  
Mocha → 70  
Matcha → 90

And compare within seconds.

Currently, every comparison requires:

Open product → Read price → Close → Open another product → Close → And so on.

### Customer Impact

- Slow scanning.
- Prevents comparison.
- Adds a click with no value.
- Creates the impression that the website is "hiding the price."
- Becomes very tiring when a category contains 20 items.

### Business Impact

Very direct: users may close the menu instead of continuing to browse.

# Problems Across the Overall Website Experience

Looking at the journey as a whole rather than each screenshot individually, there are eight core problems:

### 1. The Information Architecture Does Not Start with User Intent

The two strongest intents are likely:

**Show me the menu**  
Or  
**Where are you?**

But the website starts with a brand-heavy experience and continues through many sections.

---

### 2. Discovery Works Better Than Decision-Making

Users can find out that MARO has:

- Coffee.
- Desserts.
- Offers.
- Branches.

But when they reach the stage of asking:

> What exactly should I order?

The quality drops sharply.

---

### 3. The Menu Does Not Support Comparison

This is the most serious funnel break.

Without visible prices, images, and descriptions:

**Browse → Compare → Choose**

Breaks down at the Compare stage.

---

### 4. Scroll Fatigue Is Evident

The website is long because of:

- Large spacing.
- Big headings.
- Big cards.
- Story.
- Branch cards.
- Value cards.
- Long menu.

Not because it contains a huge amount of information.

---

### 5. The Actual Screen Area Available for Content Is Small

Safari chrome + sticky MARO header + occasional category navigation + Talabat floating button.

On an iPhone, this leaves a substantial portion of the screen unavailable for content.

---

### 6. There Is No Clear End to the Conversion Journey

A good journey should end in one of the following:

**Choose → Order**  
Or  
**Choose → Visit**

Currently:

**Choose → Learn the price → Close modal.**

---

### 7. The Trust Layer Is Incomplete

Although there are three real addresses linked to Google Maps, which is positive and useful, the visible journey lacks:

- Hours.
- Direct phone.
- WhatsApp.
- Reviews.
- Real café imagery.

The current branches are indeed linked to Google Maps. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="7"}

---

### 8. Visual Branding Is Stronger Than Product Presentation

Black + gold + typography give MARO a clear character.

But the products themselves are presented through:

**Text + Emojis.**

In other words, the container feels more premium than what is being sold inside it.

## Final Conclusion of the Current Audit

The main problem is **not that the website looks bad**.

On the contrary: the black/gold palette, typography, rounded cards, and overall direction give it character.

The problem is that most of the design effort went into **presenting MARO as a brand**, while the business-critical experience—**choosing a product from the menu**—is the least mature.

The biggest leakage points, in logical order, are:

**Enter website → Lots of content → Reach menu → Difficult category navigation → Items without information → Click to learn price → Sparse modal → No clear next action.**

Fixing this is what will turn the website from **a café landing page** into **a genuine digital menu that helps sell**.

## Summary Before the Details

The website has a **relatively clear visual identity**, but its current UX is not built around the customer's most important question:

> **"What do you offer? How much does it cost? What does it look like? And where do I order it or visit?"**

The biggest issue is that the website devotes enormous space to the brand story, sections, whitespace, and navigation, while **the product itself—the menu—is the weakest part of the experience**.

More seriously, the menu hides each item's price behind a click, shows no product image or description in the list, and offers a very sparse detail modal. As a result, users cannot even compare three drinks quickly.

The current homepage is arranged as follows: Hero → Offers → Branches → Menu preview → About → Full Menu, with Talabat for ordering and Google Maps for branches. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

## Final Conclusion of the Current Audit

The main problem is **not that the website looks bad**.

On the contrary: the black/gold palette, typography, rounded cards, and overall direction give it character.

The problem is that most of the design effort went into **presenting MARO as a brand**, while the business-critical experience—**choosing a product from the menu**—is the least mature.

The biggest leakage points, in logical order, are:

**Enter website → Lots of content → Reach menu → Difficult category navigation → Items without information → Click to learn price → Sparse modal → No clear next action.**

Fixing this is what will turn the website from **a café landing page** into **a genuine digital menu that helps sell**.
