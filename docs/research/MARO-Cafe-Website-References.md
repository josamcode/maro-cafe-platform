# MARO: Cafe Website References for Design and Conversion

Reviewed: September 27, 2026

## Recommendation

Use WatchHouse for visual restraint, Maman for product appetite, The Grounds for the journey to a visit, Blank Street for signature drink campaigns, and The Coffee Club for clear upselling. EL&N provides a particularly relevant example of an in-store menu entry point.

The twelve references below are ranked by usefulness to MARO, not by measured commercial performance. Several specific cafe or location pages are more relevant than their brands' homepages.

## Method and evidence boundary

Reviewed live desktop pages visually and read official menu, location, ordering, and loyalty information where relevant. Selection considered product desire, a clear next action, practical visit information, relevant additional purchases, and reasons to return. These are qualitative conversion assessments: public interface design does not establish actual sales uplift. No independently verified website conversion results were found for this shortlist. Mobile usability, speed, accessibility, checkout completion, and in-store operations were not audited.

Campaigns and offers are snapshots of the review date. Regional sites can differ. The conversion explanations and MARO applications below are recommendations inferred from the observed interfaces.

## 1. WatchHouse

**Study:** [Homepage](https://watchhouse.com/) and [Visit](https://watchhouse.com/pages/visit).

**Observed:** Restrained cream backgrounds, generous architectural photography, and a prominent route to discovering its cafes. The visit information combines branch imagery with operational details and amenities.

**Conversion logic:** An attractive setting creates desire; specific branch information gives that desire an actionable destination.

**Apply to MARO:** A confident hero, real interiors, and branch cards showing the information needed to choose a visit. Pair the visual story with a clearly visible menu route.

**Do not copy:** The extensive retail/subscription navigation or dependence on rotating hero content. MARO's visit action should remain visible regardless of the featured campaign.

## 2. Maman

**Study:** [Homepage](https://mamannyc.com/) and [Locations](https://mamannyc.com/locations).

**Observed:** Large close-up drink photography, a seasonal collaboration, food occasions, and ordering prompts. Its soft blue and cream presentation ties products to a recognizable cafe atmosphere.

**Conversion logic:** Customers can imagine what they will consume and when they would visit, before reading a brand history.

**Apply to MARO:** Photograph an actual signature drink alongside an actual dessert; connect each featured occasion to relevant products. This pairing is a proposed MARO treatment, not a claim that Maman operates a recommendation engine.

**Do not copy:** Small text, navigation hidden behind a desktop menu, or an introductory loading sequence. Product beauty should remain easy to browse.

## 3. The Grounds of Alexandria

**Study:** [The Grounds Cafe landing page](https://thegrounds.com.au/dine-in/the-grounds-cafe/).

**Observed:** A populated interior hero, booking access, menu navigation, opening hours, address, directions, and visit-related information on a dedicated cafe page.

**Conversion logic:** It answers both emotional and practical questions: why spend time here, and how do I make that visit happen?

**Apply to MARO:** Put real seating and atmosphere photography beside concise branch information. Make directions the main action when reservations are not part of the operating model.

**Do not copy:** The broader group's many events and venue routes. This specific cafe page is the useful reference; its full corporate navigation would overwhelm a small branch network.

## 4. Blank Street

**Study:** [UK homepage](https://www.blankstreet.com/en-GB), [Menu](https://www.blankstreet.com/en-GB/menu), and [Rewards](https://www.blankstreet.com/en-GB/rewards).

**Observed:** Seasonal named drinks, distinct matcha and latte ranges, store discovery, and app-related ordering/rewards routes. Prices were not visible in the public menu content inspected.

**Conversion logic:** A recognizable drink can become the reason for a first visit; a genuinely new seasonal item can create a reason to return.

**Apply to MARO:** Feature a small signature range with strong product names, images, descriptions, and visible prices. Give a seasonal release one focused campaign module.

**Do not copy:** A video-led first screen that delays useful content, hidden prices, or an app prerequisite for simple browsing.

## 5. EL&N

**Study:** [Homepage](https://elnlondon.com/) and [Wardour Street menu entry](https://elnlondon.com/pages/view-menu-ws).

**Observed:** The homepage emphasizes the venue and menu. The Wardour Street entry distinguishes app-based table ordering from browsing a digital menu and ordering with a server.

**Conversion logic:** It recognizes that someone already seated needs an efficient menu route rather than another introduction to the cafe.

**Apply to MARO:** Use a branch QR to open the inside menu directly. Start with browsing and My Picks; introduce ordering only when the operation supports it.

**Do not copy:** Persistent social distractions, oversized navigation, or the brand's pink palette without a MARO-specific reason. Its experience distinction is more useful than its surface styling.

## 6. The Coffee Club

**Study:** [Australian homepage](https://coffeeclub.com.au/).

**Observed:** Food and drink campaigns, menu discovery, store access, loyalty promotion, and a high-protein milk upgrade displayed with an additional price.

**Conversion logic:** A concrete upgrade with an explicit cost makes the extra-purchase decision easier to evaluate.

**Apply to MARO:** Show the incremental price of an available size or add-on beside the choice. Offer one relevant dessert pairing with its price and, if applicable, a real bundle saving.

**Do not copy:** Multiple campaigns competing for the first screen. A stronger MARO hierarchy would prioritize a small set of signature products and one timely offer.

## 7. Joe & The Juice

**Study:** [Homepage](https://www.joejuice.com/).

**Observed:** Product-led imagery, direct menu and app routes, store navigation, and popular-product merchandising. The visual language is bold and easy to associate with its food and drinks.

**Conversion logic:** It connects brand recognition with a concrete next step, while explaining why an app might be useful to an existing customer.

**Apply to MARO:** Make products identifiable at a glance. Keep the next action short and explicit; offer account or loyalty benefits at a moment when they have value.

**Do not copy:** Making app installation the default next step for a first-time web visitor. MARO must first make the menu and branch choice useful without registration.

## 8. Ole & Steen

**Study:** [UK homepage](https://oleandsteen.co.uk/).

**Observed:** Large bakery photography, seasonal campaigns, signature-product extensions, and prominent routes to the menu and bakery finder.

**Conversion logic:** Familiar signature products can support fresh launches without making every promotion depend on a discount.

**Apply to MARO:** Build recurring campaigns around products customers recognize. A seasonal version should make its flavor, availability, and next action clear.

**Do not copy:** Depending on a carousel for the only important message. Also label the destination accurately: a branch finder should be presented as a branch finder, not an immediately available checkout.

## 9. Paris Baguette

**Study:** [US homepage](https://parisbaguette.com/).

**Observed:** A seasonal caramel-apple collection spanning food and drinks, substantial food photography, repeated menu/order routes, and a rewards proposition.

**Conversion logic:** A coordinated collection encourages customers to consider more than one product category in a single occasion.

**Apply to MARO:** Show a drink and dessert as a coherent occasion, with each price visible. If a bundle exists, state exactly what it includes. Photography alone does not establish a bundle deal.

**Do not copy:** The full breadth of franchise, catering, retail, and consumer options in the main navigation. MARO's homepage needs fewer competing decisions.

## 10. Toby's Estate

**Study:** [Flagship Cafes & Roastery](https://www.tobysestate.com.au/flagship-cafes-roastery/).

**Observed:** Individual cafe photography and descriptions accompanied by branch details. Different locations are presented as distinct experiences, rather than interchangeable addresses.

**Conversion logic:** Customers can choose a branch for its particular atmosphere or offering as well as proximity.

**Apply to MARO:** Give each branch a real photograph and a short, factual distinction: seating, outdoor space, available menu, or another verified feature.

**Do not copy:** Retail-first navigation and a newsletter discount popup that interrupted the cafe page during inspection. Keep visit decisions free of unrelated retail prompts.

## 11. Bacha Coffee

**Study:** [Coffee Rooms & Boutiques](https://bachacoffee.com/us/en/locations).

**Observed:** Rich venue imagery, a distinction between coffee rooms and boutiques, and location information including menu, directions, opening hours, and reservation expectations. A shipping/language panel obscured part of the page on first entry.

**Conversion logic:** Premium imagery establishes the experience while operational detail reduces uncertainty about the visit.

**Apply to MARO:** Combine warm, detailed interior photography with concise information about what each branch actually offers.

**Do not copy:** International shipping selection before a local visitor can browse cafe details, or a heavy retail header. This is a reference for venue presentation, not an ideal complete MARO homepage.

## 12. Bluestone Lane

**Study:** [Cafe and Coffee Shop Locations](https://bluestonelane.com/cafe-and-coffee-shop-locations/).

**Observed:** Large branch photo cards, addresses, separate visit/order actions where available, and filters for region, venue type, and amenities.

**Conversion logic:** Customers can compare branches using both atmosphere and practical fit, then choose a relevant action.

**Apply to MARO:** Show all three branches directly with a few meaningful feature labels. Keep menu and directions specific to the selected branch.

**Do not copy:** A large-chain filtering system for three branches, or the homepage's mix of wholesale and retail priorities. The location page is the stronger reference for MARO.

## What MARO should look like

A warm, contemporary cafe website with restrained typography, generous real photography, and compact decision-making components. Use the existing MARO identity as the foundation. Product photography should communicate taste and texture; venue photography should communicate what spending time there feels like.

Avoid treating premium design as a reason to hide prices or reduce text contrast. The practical interface should be as clear as the photography is attractive.

### Outside experience: convert interest into a visit

| Sequence | Content | Reference principle | Intended action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One signature drink and a glimpse of the venue, concise proposition, two clear actions | WatchHouse + Maman | Explore menu or choose branch |
| 2 | Four to six MARO Picks with names, descriptions, and real prices | Blank Street + Joe & The Juice | Inspect a product |
| 3 | One available seasonal item or relevant offer | Blank Street + Paris Baguette | Explore the featured item |
| 4 | Real seating, people, and atmosphere photos | The Grounds + Maman | Assess whether the venue fits the occasion |
| 5 | Three branch cards with hours, menu, and directions | WatchHouse + Bluestone Lane | Choose a branch and get directions |
| 6 | Genuine customer feedback with source and branch context | MARO-specific evidence required | Resolve remaining hesitation |
| 7 | A repeated visit action and a discreet future rewards benefit | Clear action hierarchy | Plan a visit; optionally keep in touch |

Use a stable first-screen message instead of requiring visitors to wait through carousel slides. On mobile, prototype persistent menu and branch actions and validate that they do not obscure content.

### Inside experience: help guests choose and increase relevant purchases

QR entry should retain the branch context and show the menu immediately. Use searchable categories, images, prices, availability, and a small signature selection. Add one relevant pairing when a guest examines a product; show the combined cost transparently. My Picks can support selection before digital ordering exists.

EL&N is the closest structural reference for the inside entry. Blank Street informs drink presentation; The Coffee Club informs upgrade clarity. These ideas should inform a purpose-built menu rather than sending seated guests through the outside landing page.

### After the visit: earn a return

Present loyalty as a customer benefit after meaningful engagement. Phone verification alone does not verify a purchase. Since MARO's POS is currently unknown, transaction-linked stamps, purchase segmentation, and spend-based rewards require an operational verification method before launch. Basic menu browsing and branch discovery can proceed independently.

## Evidence quality: one useful case study with an important limitation

[The Talitha redesign case study](https://www.rodriwu.com/case/talitha) describes a local-cafe focus and reports a 33% increase in cafe order value. This is the designer's report, not independently audited evidence that the website caused the increase. The current [Talitha domain](https://talithacoffee.com/) showed a launching-soon page during review, so it is excluded from the twelve live references.

## How to establish that MARO's implementation sells

- Track menu views, product engagement, branch selection, and directions clicks as intent signals. A directions click is not a confirmed visit.
- Measure paid order value and relevant add-on attachment using verified orders when available. Saving My Picks is not a purchase.
- Measure second purchase and return intervals only when transactions can be associated reliably with customers.
- Compare focused changes using an experiment where feasible. Otherwise use clearly labeled before/after analysis and account for campaigns, seasonality, and store changes.

The first design review should concentrate on five references: WatchHouse, Maman, The Grounds, Blank Street, and EL&N. Keep the remaining seven as references for specific components and commercial mechanisms.
